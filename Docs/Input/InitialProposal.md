# Recipe Codex: Initial Proposal
I have an idea for an app that would help me collect, store, browse, and cook recipes from my many cookbooks, apps, websites, etc. The main problems I’m trying to solve is access to recipes found in my cookbooks when I’m not at home, enable the ability to scan/add an entire new cookbook when I buy one quickly and easily, consolidate recipes across many sources, the ability to add tags/categories/metadata/ratings/notes to improve search/browse, simplify ingredient management and shopping, the ability to plan an entire menu with multiple recipes included, and provide a guided cooking mode that follows along with me and across recipes in a menu. 
## Thoughts on scope, functionality, etc
- Lets assume for the moment I will be the only user of this app, but we should leave the door open for me to be able to bring it to market if I choose. That means for the moment we should only be concerned with making it work on my devices — an iPhone 17 pro, a 13 inch iPad Pro, and potentially my M4 Max MacBook Pro (though the latter should come later and only if needed). 
- App should follow all Apple human interface and iOS liquid glass design/developer guidelines. Ensure you have a comprehensive underatanding of both, from foundations, to patterns, components, layout, inputs, to any included tecnologies. References found below.
  - https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/
  - https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/foundations
  - https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/patterns
  - https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/components

  - https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/technologies
  - https://www.figma.com/community/file/1651309003795292092/ios-and-ipados-27

- Recipe input methods should be comprehensive and cover a range of use cases:
  - If importing from another app or web page, it would be great if the share prompt within those apps (such as NYTCooking) and/or safari enabled an option to share with our app to import it. 
  - User can manually add recipe they created or has no source manually and step by step. Unknown details, such as cook time, can be left blank by the user and automatically filled once the user cooks it and the app can gauge the value (such as cook time) or solicit feedback on outcome (such as serving yield)
  - Users can add recipes from a cookbook one off by scanning the page with their devices camera. 
    - Scanning should be as quick and easy as possible while ensuring accurate text capture. App should notify user if image is blurry or OCR is unreliable. 
    - App should warn the user if the recipe references another in the book, such as a recipe for ravioli listing pasta dough in the ingrediants, with the recipe for pasta dough found elsewhere in the book. In such cases, the app should maintain a separate record of the referenced sub-recipe, but also pull in its details and ingredients in the parent recipe and cook experience flow. 
    - App should prompt the user to capture cookbook info, potentially by scanning ISBN, and record the page it comes from. 
    - App should capture any background recipe details or acompanying story but minimize these by default so they don't clutter the recipe page in-app. 
  - The app should facilitate adding the recipes from an entire cookbook in the easiest manner possible for the user in one session, if the user chooses to do so. I was thinking that the app could prompt the user to begin by scanning the ISBN, and the app could then use that to fetch title details, cover image, and any other relevant details. It could then walk the user through scanning the contents with their devices camera page by page, or maybe starting with the index for the app to create a punchlist of all included recipes. OCR should be used to create generate a clean text version of the recipe details, but the user should also be able to capture photos of the cooked food that accompany many recipes, which the app should clean up and enhance to be used as a cover image for the recipe. 
    - User should also be able to capture a photo of the their own cooked food and add it to the recipe, replacing the cover image or not. 
    - App should make note of linked or sub recipes referenced in a recipe 
  - ingredient quantity should support standard cooking units (cups, ounces, tablespoons, etc), as well as metric (milileters, etc), weights across imperial and metric, and auto convert where possible. 
  - The app should maintain a record of when and where it was added from, the author/chef, rating from the source if available, as well as a link back to the source if available. 
    - Where available (such as website or app recipes), the app should scrape the comments and summarize to pull out any relevant insights.
- Track comprehensive metadata, autogenerated from the recipe where possible for things like dietary restrictions (vegan, vegetarian, etc), course (starter, main, dessert, etc), complexity/time required, etc, as well as enable user provided rating and notes. App should also call out if a recipe has never been cooked before, its cook history and where it was made, as well as track real cooking time where possible to refine the estimated time required provided by most recipes
  - App should generate estimated calories and macros based on ingrediates and serving size/recipe yield
  - Allow user to edit recipe and keep a version history with cook notes on the outcome
  - Based on complexity, time required, and tools used by the recipe, the app should infer it's own tags, such as if something isn't a good candidate for being made outside their own kitchen (Airbnbs are unlikely to have fully stocked kitchens), etc.
- User browse
  - Can browse by recipe, adding filters based on metadata
  - Can browse by cookbook, view all books in a library view, selecting one to view all included recipes, by section, or the index
    - App should call out if a book is incomplete, and provide details on what has been recorded and what is missing where possible. 
    - Cookbook library view should be sort/filterable for things such as cuisine type, author/chef, etc
  - Can search based on keyword or metadata, including fuzzy search referencing cook history (eg "that vegetable dish I made a few months ago in Seattle")
- user profile
  - App should allow user to input details about their setup and profficiency that subsequently customizes app behavior.
    -  enable user to add/manage a library of stocked pantry items the app should assume are on hand (though also enable user to mark a stocked pantry item as out of stock and add it to the shopping list).
    - App should also allow user to add/manage the tools available in their kitchen, such as stand mixer, blender, sous vide circulator, oven (and how many), food processor, etc. App should surface gaps if a recipe calls for a tool not in the users tool library
    - user proficiency should inform how many concurrent tasks the app schedules to be completed at once, assuming a more profficient user is able to juggle multiple things at once and keep track of them, and/or is capable of executing more complex techniques
- Create a menu
  - App should allow user to create an upcoming event with details on guest count, dietary restrictions, and any other relevant details
  - User can then build a menu manually with search/browse, with the app suggesting potential gaps such as other dishes needed to balance the menu (this menu could use an appetizer, or vegetable side dish, or isn't enough food for the amount of people), warn a user if they add a recipe that hasn't been cooked before or if multiple recipes added are very involved/complex. If the user adds a recipe that references another sub-recipe (such as pasta dough for our earlier ravioli example), app should automatically add the sub-recipe to the menu.
    - User should be able to add multiuple potential recipes that fill the same niche and finalize later
    - User should be able to adjust recipe serving yield to make a larger quantity, and the app should also adjust ingredient quantity
      - Adjusted ingredient quantity should autoconvert to the simplest equivalent measurement, such as 4 tablespoons becoming a quarter cup.
    - user should be able to set target doneness for relevant meats (rare, medium rare, etc), which the app converts into temperature targets based on the protein type, resting period after removal from the heat, etc
  - Potenaitlly AI-driven (Apple foundational models) 'create a menu' experience where user can describe the event, detail the audience, what they're looking for, and any other thoughts, and the app can parse the library of recipes to suggest a balanced menu that meets user needs. Interactive/conversational experience allows user to refine, replace suggestions with alternatives, etc.
  - Once finalized, app should: 
    - create a complete list of all required ingredients, aggregating the same ingredient needed in multiple recipes, to create a shopping list
      - Users should be able to add ingredients from a recipe or menu to a shopping list, as well as manage a library of stocked pantry items the app should assume are on hand (though also enable user to mark a stocked pantry item as out of stock and add it to the shopping list).
        - Ability to connect to available grocery store APIs would be a plus, enabling a user to easily go from the in-app shopping list to a ready to submit whole foods order, calling out gaps where items are unavailable. Not a firm necesity however
      - Shopping list should automatically group similar items likely to be found together in a grocery store to make shopping easier, and separate specialty items that likely need to be sourced individually
      - User should be able to add shopping list stops (Such as whole foods, butcher, fishmonger), and app should auto-organize the ingredient list based on the items best suited to be bought from that store.
    - generate a prep and execution workback shedule, based on the cook time in the recipe and any make before/waiting steps included in the recipe (such as if a dough needs to be made a day ahead and rest in the fridge for 24 hours) that the user can then edit, managing the schedule and to do list across multiuple days. Tasks that can be done ahead (such as making a dough) should be easy to move around from one day/time to the next, but the user should be warned if they're overloading a day/time, or if moving a task to a specific day would cause an issue for a recipe (this task requires the dough be rested in the fridge for 24 hours; executing it at noon the day of the event wouldn't allow sufficient time). 
- Cook mode
  - Cook mode should be the execution version of the workback schedule, guiding the user through task prep, completion, etc.
    - Should provide a high level view of task breakdown across the schedule, dependancies, interconnected tasks, etc, allowing user to edit/refine the plan.
  - track menu execution across recipes, with a suggested task flow that flows from one to the other seamlessly, working back from guest arrival time and eat time. 'Wash the vegetables for the side dish' can be followed with 'prep the ingredients for the bolognese' if that is the most efficient task grouping to facilitate proper mise en place, batching like tasks together, and clean as you go methodology. App should also optimize for prepping and cleaning up as much as possible before guests arrive to make final execution and serving while hosting as simple as possible. 
    - App should track progress against schedule and maintain progress status against plan, such as if the user is running 30 mins behind based on when the guests will arrive
    - App should suggest pot size where relevant based on ingredient quantity/volume that will be added to it
  - App should allow user to interact with cooking mode (such as marking a task as complete, moving on to the next step, etc) in multiple modalities in case hands are occupied or too dirty to tough the screen. Could be voice interaction with siri, or less refined touch interactions that can be done with other body parts. 
  - Prompts user to create a timer when relevant and manage multiple timers across a recipe or menu
  - Allow user to move between tasks than can be done simultaneously, such as begin browning the meat in a pan, and then start chopping the vegetables for the next step or another recipe while the meat browns
    - App should notify user if a previous or in progress task needs checking on ('the roast should be almost ready; check its temperature)
    - App should prompt and collect real-world progress information and subsequently provide informed guidance based on that data. Such as: The roast should be almost done; check its temperature. User checks temp and inputs 120 against a target of 130 for medium rare with a 10 minute resting period. Based on the protein type and cut size/shape/thermal dynamics in the heat environment, app informs user it should be about x minutes more the prompts the user to check again when the time is elapsed. 
      - Similarly, app should prompt user to check the temp of meat and seafood for temp to confirm minimum doneness based on food safety standards. 
  - App should coordinate tasks to maximize what can be prepped and done ahead while ensuring the finished product is as well executed as possible. For example, app should know not to suggest pasta be cooked and prepped an hour before serving, as it would no longer be hot and fresh for guests when they sit down. Similarly, App should account for plating order and time, guiding the user through prep, cook, hold, plating, and serving steps so everything lands together. 
  - Guided cook mode should be optimized for device size 
- Enable easy export/sharing that doesn't require recipient to have the app installed. PDF, notes?

## General thoughts on how I like to host:
How do I host? I thought a bit about that recently, because in my mind being a good host (from my perspective) can be very much at odds with some of my other personality traits. I love hosting dinner parties, and when I was in Seattle, I help a weekly Sunday supper for my close knit friend group, generally 8-10 people. The food was always made from scratch by me. 

A great host is generous, accommodating, easy going. They should meet every (reasonable) guest need quickly and with minimal fuss, anticipating those needs to ensure resources are on hand to fulfill. Gluten free? No problem. Don’t drink alcohol? I have a mean mocktail recipe and everything I need is in the fridge. They can curate an environment intentionally that encourages guests to leave their baggage at the door, set aside whatever thoughts were occupying their mind before crossing the threshold, and just enjoy themselves. That frequently comes down to refining the physical space the host and guests occupy, optimizing the guest list to ensure the right mix of people and lubricating/facilitating their conversation, optimizing the timing and so on. Most importantly, however, I think they have to put guests at ease, which means they have to make their very effortful endeavor to be a good host appear effortless and simple to their guest. No one want to show up at someone’s house for a dinner party only to watch their host sweat away in the kitchen trying to finish everything on time, delivering only clipped responses when guests try to engage them in conversation. That just makes people feel bad, and like the burden you’ve taken on for them is too great. Meeting your guests needs should never be interpreted as onerous or complicated; rather, it’s something you’re delighted to do and capable of making the delivery of complex needs feel simple. 

That last bit creates tension with my desire to challenge myself. I love complicated recipes, especially new ones, that make me learn new techniques and sample new flavors. I also set an extremely high bar for myself and success is only met through excellence: the food must be notably great, the banter must flow easily, the jokes on point, the wine paired with precision. Making a 20 ingredients, 30 step recipe feel effortless, especially when it’s only one of six planned for the table, all of which are executed by only one person, is no simple thing. It means first and foremost planning and preparation are critical. Everything that can be prepped ahead of time is, so the chives are already chopped, in the fridge, and the kitchen is spotless when guests arrive. It requires a sixth sense about timing and coordinating cooking to land everything on the table at the same time that only comes with experience. Every recipe is partially memorized so I alway know the next three steps, and always tested at least once before it makes the menu where other people are the audience. I preplan simple tasks held in reserve in case someone insists on helping me cook, though if I do my job right that doesn’t ever happen. It requires careful management of my demeanor to not get too lost in a task and maintain focus on my guests, keeping half of mind on the stove and whatever’s left on ensuring glasses are kept full. 

Making people feel welcomed, at ease, and meeting their every need while making it feel effortless is, in short, an extremely effortful endeavor.
